Minimization and DeMorgan

Each output of a PAL, GAL or CPLD macrocell is an OR of a fixed number of product terms. cupl expands every equation into a sum of products and then minimizes it so that it fits. The documentation file (-x) shows the result of every output, and its symbol table how many product terms each one uses and has.

Minimization levels

FlagMethodUse
-m0noneKeeps the product terms as written, e.g. to keep redundant terms that avoid hazards. Designs often no longer fit.
-m1quick (default)Fast and good for most designs.
-m2Quine-McCluskeyExact minimum for each output; slow for many inputs.
-m3PrestoHeuristic; handles larger functions than -m2.
-m4EspressoHeuristic; usually the best result for large designs.

In LinCUPL the level is chosen in Options > Compiler Options.

The MIN statement

MIN variable[.extension] = level;
MIN [list] = level;

MIN sets the level for single outputs, overriding the command line. With MIN z = 0; the equation z = a & b # a & !b; keeps both terms, while every other output is minimized to the level of the compile (z = a).

DeMorgan and polarity

Many devices can invert an output. An output whose complement has fewer product terms can then be built from the complement: y = !(a & b & c) needs three terms (!a # !b # !c) but its complement only one. The flags

FlagEffect
-kboptimizes the product term usage by choosing the polarity of each output
-kdapplies DeMorgan's theorem to all pins and nodes; designs can then need more terms than before
-ksforces product term sharing between outputs (in devices that can share terms)
-kxkeeps XOR equations as XOR instead of expanding them, for the virtual device and fitters with XOR gates

An output built from its complement keeps its function: the fuse map inverts it again, also for equations that start with a NOT and for pins declared active low.

The original CUPL inverts some of these outputs once too often with -kb and -kd (on the GAL16V8 as on the 22V10 and ATV750), so its fuse maps with these flags can differ from LinCUPL's. On the ATV750 and ATV2500 it often stops with [1018cb] program error under -kb, where LinCUPL compiles. Neither program should be given -kb or -kd for designs with toggle (.t) flip-flops: complementing a toggle input does not complement the output.

An exclusive OR of n signals expands to 2n-1 product terms, which is why wide XORs (parity, comparators) often do not fit. Split them over nodes, or use a device whose macrocells have XOR gates.

When a design does not fit

  • excessive number of product terms: try -m4 or -kb, move logic to a node, or choose an output pin whose macrocell has more terms (on a 22V10 the middle pins have the most).
  • Look at the .doc file to see which output is too large.
  • For a CPLD the fitter handles the placement and can split large functions itself.
